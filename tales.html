Tales from Savoy

Stories drawn from the archive — the people and moments that made this town

Home › Tales from Savoy

The archive holds thousands of documents, photographs, and newspaper clippings spanning a century and a half. These tales draw on that record to tell the stories that gave Savoy its character — the deals, the disasters, the institutions, and the people who kept building something worth remembering.

1872  ·  Founding
Forty Acres and a Name

A Mississippi-born adventurer gave forty acres to a railroad and asked for one thing in return: that the town bear his name. The deed was filed in Bonham on October 29, 1872.

1880  ·  Disaster
The Night the Cyclone Came

At 10:10 p.m. on Friday, May 28, 1880, a tornado tore through the center of Savoy. The hotel keeper later said it was over in five seconds.

1876–1890  ·  Education
The Colonel's College

For fourteen years, the Savoy Male and Female College was one of the genuine intellectual centers of North Texas. Then the building burned, and the last class was never dismissed.

Read the tale
1890s–1940s  ·  Commerce
The Press That Wouldn't Die

An 1830 Washington hand press printed the Savoy Star for forty-one years. When it finally stopped, the Sherman Democrat bought it as a museum piece.

Read the tale
1954  ·  Disaster
Saturday Before Dawn

The fire in downtown Savoy started around 4:30 in the morning, when most of the town was still asleep. By the time anyone could respond, it had already taken three buildings.

1878–2005  ·  Community
The Bell on Fowler Street

Col. Savoy gave the town his name. He also gave the lot on Fowler Street where the Methodist church was built in 1878. More than 125 years later, the bell still rings.

May 28, 1880  ·  Eyewitness
Five Seconds

The hotel keeper was asked what the cyclone was like. He thought about it and said: five seconds. That was all the time it gave the town before it was gone.

May 28, 1880  ·  Eyewitness
The Doctor in the Dark

Dr. McKee was a physician in Savoy on the night the cyclone struck. His wife broke her arm. The seminary became a hospital. Someone in Bonham thought: send ice.

May 28, 1880  ·  Eyewitness
The Premonition

On the evening of May 28, 1880, a Savoy blacksmith moved his family out of their house. He couldn't say why — he just felt something was wrong. Two hours later, the cyclone took the house.

May 28–29, 1880  ·  The Cyclone
Rilla

The cyclone threw her forty or fifty yards clear of the wreckage. She was not found until daylight. They brought her to the seminary, and she died the next morning.

1880 / 1937 / 1993  ·  Memory
Cows Were Found with Their Feathers

Fifty-seven years after the cyclone, survivors still told the strange stories. Two doctors blown naked. Balls of electricity rolling across a floor. Chicken feathers driven into cattle hides.

1930  ·  Memory
The Anniversary Poem

On the fiftieth anniversary of the cyclone, a woman named M. May published a poem in the local paper. It began: "'Twas in the year of 1880 on the 28th of May."

1884  ·  The Colonel
The Colonel and the Indian

In 1884, Col. Savoy boarded a train carrying California gold nuggets from his 1849 trip. He met a man he'd once known in Indian Territory who pulled out a gold nugget of his own.

1880–1895  ·  Memory & Record
What the Register Lost

The original church records of Savoy's Methodist congregation were lost in the 1880 cyclone. When a new register was compiled from memory, someone wrote in the margin: "probably in some instances incorrect."

Mid-20th Century  ·  Community
Eula Phillips, Stringer

For years, Eula Phillips filed news dispatches from Savoy to the county paper. She wasn't paid much. She was paid attention. The whole town knew that if something happened, Eula would write it down.

1969–1990s  ·  Industry
The Super Sack Town

In 1969, a company put a plant in Savoy and started making bags. By 1988, 177 employees cheered at a groundbreaking. The name became so ubiquitous that competitors' customers started asking for "some super sacks."
